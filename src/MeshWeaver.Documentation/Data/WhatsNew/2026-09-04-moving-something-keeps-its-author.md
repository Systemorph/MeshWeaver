# Moving something keeps its author and its dates

Moving a page — from the node menu or with the `move` command — used to record it as if it had just
been written from scratch by whoever did the move. The creator, the creation date, the last-edited
date and the last editor were all replaced with "you, just now", and not only on the item you
moved: everything underneath it was rewritten the same way. One tidy-up of a client folder was
enough to make eighty documents, a commercial proposal among them, look like they had been authored
that minute by the person reorganising them. Nothing could recover the real values afterwards.

A move changes where something lives. It does not change who wrote it, and it is not an edit, so all
four stamps now travel with the item and with everything under it. The path and the namespace change
— that is the point of a move — and nothing else does.

Copying is deliberately different, and now says so consistently: a copy is a new item, so it is
recorded as created by you, at the moment you copied it, while the original keeps its own history
untouched.
